Why the Gulf war is a Kerala story

Faultline Analysis  |  8 November 2026  |  4 min read

Most coverage of the Iran war treats the Gulf as an oil story. For India, and for Kerala above all, it is also a story about people, wages and the money that keeps millions of households afloat.

A state built on Gulf wages

About half of all Indian migrants live in the six Gulf Cooperation Council states. Kerala is the most exposed part of that link. According to the Reserve Bank of India’s latest remittances survey, Kerala received 19.7% of India’s inward remittances in 2023–24, about $24 billion of a $118.7 billion total, second only to Maharashtra.

The Gulf’s share of India’s remittances has been falling as more Indians work in the US, UK and other advanced economies; it stood at 33.7% in 2023–24. But for many Kerala households, especially those of lower-paid workers, the Gulf is still where the money comes from.

Three ways the war reaches home

Safety. Gulf states hosting US forces are on Iran’s target list, and shipping in and around Hormuz is under regular attack. An Indian seafarer was killed in an attack near Oman in early November, and the UN counts 25 seafarers killed in Hormuz incidents since the war began. Each escalation raises the question of whether workers can stay, and whether India could evacuate them at scale.

Jobs and remittances. If fighting spreads to the Gulf states themselves, construction, hospitality and logistics jobs are the first to go. A sustained drop in Gulf remittances would hit household spending, bank deposits and the construction sector across Kerala.

Prices and compliance. With Hormuz down to a handful of transits a day, oil prices feed straight into India’s import bill. And on 5 November the US sanctioned Indian and Turkish firms accused of helping Iranian tankers, a reminder that Indian businesses can be caught in the sanctions net.

Faultline view: We rate India Elevated overall, but its Gulf exposure, covering energy, diaspora and remittances, as High. The single biggest variable for Kerala is whether the war spreads from shipping lanes to the Gulf states’ own cities.

What to watch

Any Iranian strike on a Gulf city with a large Indian workforce; Indian government evacuation advisories; monthly remittance and deposit data from Kerala’s banks; and whether Qatar’s plan to reopen Hormuz gains traction.
