How to read a listing

The list is kept by the Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force, chaired by the Department of Homeland Security. Goods made wholly or partly by a listed entity are presumed to be made with forced labour and are barred from US entry unless the importer proves otherwise with clear and convincing evidence. A listing is a government determination, not a court finding, and listed companies can ask to be removed.

Forced labour in Xinjiang

Section 2(d)(2)(B)(i): entities in Xinjiang that mine, produce or manufacture goods wholly or in part with forced labour.

Labour transfers

Section 2(d)(2)(B)(ii): entities working with the Xinjiang government to recruit, transport, transfer, harbour or receive forced labour or Uyghurs, Kazakhs, Kyrgyz or members of other persecuted groups out of Xinjiang.

Sourcing from Xinjiang

Section 2(d)(2)(B)(v): entities, including the XPCC, that source material from Xinjiang or from people working with the Xinjiang government or the XPCC on “poverty alleviation”, “pairing assistance” or other government labour schemes.

Method and sources

Names, aliases and listing bases come from the official list. Profiles add open-source research, and every claim beyond the official text is attributed. Screening priority is a Faultline judgment about how urgently a counterparty should check its exposure, not a finding about the company.

How screening priority works

We start from the listing basis (forced labour or labour transfers rank above sourcing), then raise priority when the entity is in Xinjiang, the listing covers subsidiaries or a wider corporate family, the sector is a US enforcement priority, or other US measures also apply. Severe, high and elevated are relative: every entity on this list carries real exposure.

Open-source tools behind the profiles

Chinese corporate registries (the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System), exchange filings on CNINFO and HKEX, OpenCorporates and GLEIF for ownership; US import records for trade links; and research from Sheffield Hallam University, ASPI and the Uyghur Human Rights Project.
