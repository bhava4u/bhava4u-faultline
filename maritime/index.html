Shadow fleet watch

Ships seen in our monitored zones that OpenSanctions flags as sanctioned, part of a shadow fleet, or detained by port-state inspectors, matched by IMO or MMSI. A match is a lead to check, not proof of wrongdoing.

Possible ship-to-ship transfers

Pairs of near-stationary ships within 500 metres of each other where at least one is a tanker. Busy anchorages create many such pairs, so the ones involving a listed ship matter most.

    How this works

    Sources, update times and the limits worth knowing before acting on anything here.

    Ship positions

    From the free AISStream.io feed of AIS transponder messages, sampled for three minutes every 30 minutes inside the monitored zones drawn on the globe. Coverage comes from shore receivers, so ships far offshore may be missing. Positions older than three hours are dropped.

    Sanctions and listed ships

    Matched by IMO number, or by MMSI where no IMO is broadcast, against the OpenSanctions maritime dataset, refreshed daily. Port-state bans and detentions are shown separately from sanctions. Ships can change name and flag but keep their IMO number.

    Chokepoint traffic

    Daily transit counts from IMF PortWatch, which estimates crossings from satellite AIS. PortWatch data runs a few days behind. Normal levels use a pre-crisis window named on each chokepoint.

    Outlooks, premiums and incidents

    Written by Faultline analysts from open-source reporting, with sources linked. Premiums are published broker indications, not quotes. The voyage cost check is a rough comparison for discussion, not advice.

    GPS interference

    Jamming and spoofing are common in the Gulf, the Red Sea and the Black Sea. We count position jumps faster than 60 knots as a sign of interference. Some ships also switch off AIS, so an empty area is not proof that no ships are there.

    Updates

    Live data refreshes about every 30 minutes and this page reloads it every 10 minutes while open. Analyst content follows the main Faultline review cycle.
